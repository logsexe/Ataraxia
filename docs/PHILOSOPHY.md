# Ataraxia product philosophy

Ataraxia exists to help people use social media deliberately and then leave peacefully.

## The finite-life premise

An 80-year life contains roughly 4,174 weeks. “About 4,000 weeks” is an intentionally memorable approximation, not a prediction about any individual life. The idea should create perspective, never fear, guilt or manufactured urgency.

Attention is not merely a metric. It is the medium through which a life is experienced.

## Four principles

### Attention

Notice what is asking for the mind before giving it away. Interfaces should make limits and consequences legible.

### Intention

Every useful path should correspond to something a person meant to do: read a message, visit a chosen profile, publish something or browse a finite feed.

### Moderation

Enough is a valid ending. Ataraxia should create stopping points instead of exploiting completion bias, variable rewards or infinite continuation.

### Agency

The person remains in control. Boundaries are chosen locally and may be changed. Ataraxia supports deliberate choice; it does not pose as parental control or claim to make bypass impossible.

## Product constraints

Ataraxia must not add:

- infinite feeds, autoplay chains or swipe-to-next loops;
- streaks, shame, punishment or moral scoring;
- engagement optimisation, behavioural profiling or attention-based analytics;
- developer advertising, sponsored ranking or payment-gated wellbeing controls;
- false claims that filtering is perfect or that Meta no longer processes Instagram activity.

Ataraxia should prefer:

- explicit destinations over recommendation surfaces;
- finite sessions over endless continuation;
- calm language over alarms;
- local settings over developer accounts;
- honest limitations over absolute privacy or filtering claims;
- reflection that leads to action, not quote collections that become another feed.

## Experience rule

Philosophy must change behaviour in the product. It belongs in onboarding, mode selection, stopping points and feature decisions—not only in branding.

The app remains free. Optional donations or grants may support maintenance, but functionality must not be locked behind payment.
